What makes it different

Every claim carries an evidence label

Most food guides blur "traditionally used" with "proven". This one keeps them apart, page by page.

Established

Backed by good research and accepted by health bodies, for example folate before pregnancy.

Possible benefit

Promising studies exist, but they are small, mixed or still early.

Traditional use

Used for generations in many cultures. History is not the same as proof.

Not proven

Popular claims that research has not shown to be true.

Nothing in the book claims to cure, treat or prevent infertility, prostate disease or any other condition. A full chapter explains what food cannot fix and who can.

Straight talk

What this book will not do

Food supports health. It does not replace medical care, and no honest guide can promise otherwise.

  • It does not promise to cure infertility, unblock tubes, raise sperm counts in every case or shrink a prostate.
  • It does not say that natural means risk-free. Allergies, medicine interactions and supplement limits are covered.
  • It does not replace a doctor, a semen analysis or a PSA discussion.

When to see a doctor

Trying for 12 months without success (6 months if the woman is 35 or over), difficulty passing urine, blood in urine or semen, or a testicular lump. The book lists these signs and the questions to bring to your appointment.

Questions

Before you buy

What format is it, and how do I get it?

It is a PDF in A4 size, 78 pages. After payment you receive a download link. It reads well on a laptop or tablet and prints cleanly.

Will this help us conceive?

Healthy eating supports general health, and some nutrients matter for fertility. The book cannot promise a pregnancy. If you have been trying for a year, or have known conditions, it explains when to get checked.

Is it a cure or treatment for prostate problems?

No. It explains what nutrition may support and what it cannot do. Symptoms need a doctor.

Is it for both men and women?

Yes. There are sections on male fertility, female fertility and prostate health, and the meals suit couples cooking together.

Are the ingredients easy to find?

The recipes use everyday foods, and the timetable chapter lists swaps for anything hard to find or costly. Frozen vegetables and berries are suggested throughout.

Can I use it if I take medicine or have a condition?

Many foods are fine, but some affect medicines such as blood thinners. The safety chapter lists known interactions. Please speak to your doctor or pharmacist before major changes.

What sources does the book use?

Guidance from health bodies such as the WHO, NIH and the urology and fertility societies, plus published studies, all listed in the bibliography. Please check the sources yourself and ask your clinician about your own situation.
